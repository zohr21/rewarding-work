## How it works

Rather than a to-do list that waits for you to choose, you give each piece of work a place in time. "Write proposal" becomes "9:00–10:30 write proposal". Similar small tasks (email, messages, admin) get grouped into one block rather than scattered across the day.

This does three things: it forces you to confront how much actually fits in a day, it removes the constant "what next?" decision, and it protects focused time from being eaten by small stuff.

Things will run over and interruptions will happen. When they do, you don't abandon the plan — you re-block the rest of the day. That's expected, not failure.

## Try it now

1. Take 10 minutes. List what you want to get done today (or tomorrow).
2. Assign each item a block on a calendar or a sheet of paper, with realistic lengths.
3. Add at least one buffer block and real breaks — including lunch.
4. Group small tasks into a single "shallow work" block.
5. At the end of the day, compare plan and reality. Adjust tomorrow's block lengths accordingly.
