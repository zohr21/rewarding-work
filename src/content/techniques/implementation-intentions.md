## How it works

Goals like "study more" or "exercise" state *what* you want but not *when*, *where* or *how*. When the moment comes, you have to decide on the spot — and that's where things slip.

An implementation intention links a specific cue to a specific action:

> **If** it's 8:00 and I've sat down at my desk, **then** I will open my notes and write for 10 minutes.

Once the plan is set, the situation itself becomes a reminder, and the action starts more automatically. You can also plan for obstacles:

> **If** I feel the urge to check my phone while studying, **then** I will put it in the other room.

## Try it now

1. Choose one thing you keep meaning to do.
2. Pick a specific situation that happens reliably: a time, a place, or something you already do ("after I make coffee…").
3. Write it as one sentence: **If** [situation], **then** I will [small, concrete action].
4. Add one obstacle plan: **If** [likely obstacle], **then** I will [response].
5. Say both plans out loud once, or write them where you'll see them.
