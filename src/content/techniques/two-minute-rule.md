## How it works

There are two versions, and both are useful.

**The original (David Allen):** when a new task appears and it would take less than two minutes, do it immediately. Tracking, filing and remembering it would take longer than just doing it.

**The starting version (James Clear):** when you're avoiding something big, shrink it to a version that takes two minutes. "Write the essay" becomes "open the document and write one sentence". "Go running" becomes "put on running shoes". The goal is only to start; if you keep going afterwards, that's a bonus, but it isn't required.

Starting is often the biggest hurdle, and a two-minute step is small enough that there's little to resist.

## Try it now

1. Think of one task you've been putting off.
2. Write the smallest possible first step — something you could finish in two minutes.
3. Do only that step, right now.
4. When you're done, you're allowed to stop. Notice whether you want to continue.
